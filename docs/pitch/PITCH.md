# PULSE — pitch

**From business objective to rights-aware campaign — and automatically to a better alternative when something cannot be used.**
Challenge: Rights & Compliance · Partners: ScorePlay + TwelveLabs.

## 15-second hook

> "Sports clubs don't have a content shortage. They have a content usage problem.
> The best clip for a merch post has a rival's crest on the hoarding and a player nobody can name. Today that clip dies in an email thread.
> We don't just detect the problem. We find a way to solve it."

## 30-second product explanation

> "You type a business goal — *promote merchandise to UK supporters*. PULSE finds three real moments in the club's ScorePlay library, with exact timestamps from TwelveLabs, and builds the campaign for every channel and market.
> Then it checks every use against the club's rules. When something can't be used, it shows *where* in the clip and *which* rule — and finds a way: it tries a cut, rejects it if too little is left, proposes a replacement or a clean section, or shrinks the plan to licensed markets. Every option is re-checked by the same engine before you see it.
> A named reviewer approves, and the decision goes back to ScorePlay as a timecoded log — a dry run today."

## The differentiator (one line)

> "Other tools identify what you can't publish. PULSE finds the best way to achieve your goal anyway."

Three kinds of answer, shown live:
1. **The footage is the problem** → cut it (if enough is left), use a clean section, or replace it with another real moment (Demo 1: merch).
2. **The plan is the problem** → keep the clip, change the plan, e.g. "Limit to licensed markets: GB, US" (Demo 2: global engagement).
3. **No valid path** → say so, keep review locked, name the next step: "Request additional usage rights" (Mexico). PULSE never re-targets your goal behind your back.

## Who buys

| Buyer | Pain | What PULSE gives them |
|---|---|---|
| Head of Digital / Social lead at a club | Good clips go unused because nobody is sure they are cleared | Goal → moments → campaign → verdict → a usable alternative, without a rights expert in the loop for the easy cases |
| Commercial / partnerships team | Sponsor conflicts discovered after publishing | Partner exclusivity checked per channel × market before review; packages per commercial context (social vs sponsor activation) |
| Rights / legal team | Ad-hoc requests, no paper trail | Evidence with timestamps, the rule that applied, named sign-off, hash-chained audit receipt |
| ScorePlay (partner channel) | Assets are stored, rights are not actionable | A compliance layer on ScorePlay's public API that writes decisions back as timecoded video logs |

## Business model (no invented numbers)

- **Club subscription** per media library, with seats for producers and reviewers.
- **Distribution through ScorePlay** as an add-on for clubs already on the platform; TwelveLabs indexing as the video-intelligence layer the club already pays for or adds.
- **Rights onboarding service**: encoding a club's real licence terms as a rule package with its rights team (today every package is a fictional demo).
- We show **no ROI, revenue or conversion figures**: sales attribution is not connected and the app says so. A pilot would measure time from goal to approved campaign, share of blocked campaigns recovered, conflicts caught before publishing, and reviewer minutes per campaign.

## What is real (say it before they ask)

Real: ScorePlay assets, tags and squads (read-only); TwelveLabs moments and timestamps; two Pegasus analysis runs (the second recorded and labelled); the deterministic re-checks; reviews, receipts, audit trail.
Fictional: every rule package — "Demo policy — not verified legal clearance" on every verdict.
Not done: no video is rendered (edit plans), no ScorePlay write (dry run), previews off.
